# Melody — the theory behind arstro.solaris.compose.melody

Contents: 1 harmony and the non-chord tones · 2 scale degrees and their pull · 3 the motif and its development ·
4 phrase forms · 5 contour · 6 rhythm · 7 cadences · 8 genre notes · 9 the rubric, and why each part · 10 a worked
example (Porch Light's theme)

Numbers are scale degrees (1 = the tonic). "Strong" means beat 1 or beat 3 of a 4/4 bar, or any note a beat or longer.

---

## 1. Harmony and the non-chord tones

A melody note is heard AGAINST the chord under it. On a strong beat a chord tone sounds stable; anything else sounds
like tension that wants to move. That tension is not a mistake — it is the melody's expression — but it must be
**placed** and **resolved**.

| kind | where | how it moves | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| chord tone | anywhere | free | stable; the 3rd and 5th sing, the root is plain (it doubles the bass), the 7th and 9th are colour |
| passing tone | weak beat | step in, step on in the same direction | motion between chord tones |
| neighbour tone | weak beat | step away, step back | an ornament, a turn |
| **appoggiatura** | **strong beat** | leapt or stepped into, resolves by STEP (usually down) | the "lean" — longing; the most emotional device in pop and film |
| **suspension** | strong beat | a chord tone held over the chord change, then resolves down a step (4–3, 9–8, 7–6) | ache, then release |
| anticipation | just before a beat | the next chord's note, early | push, eagerness; everywhere in pop and EDM |
| escape tone | weak beat | step away, leap back the other way | a flick, a sigh |

Rule of practice: **≥ 80 % of strong notes on chord tones; every strong non-chord tone resolves by step.**

## 2. Scale degrees and their pull

| degree (major) | stability | pull | colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | home | — | rest, end |
| 2 | unstable | → 1 or → 3 | open, a question |
| 3 | stable | — | warmth, the "major" of major |
| 4 | unstable | → 3 | suspension, yearning |
| 5 | stable | — | strong, open |
| 6 | soft | → 5 | tenderness, nostalgia |
| 7 | the most unstable | → 1 | longing, the leading tone |

Minor: ♭3 is the sadness itself; ♭6 → 5 is the lament (the "sigh"); ♭7 is modal, folk-like, not a leading tone.
**Borrowed** tones (♭3, ♭6, ♭7 in a major key, from the iv, ♭VI and ♭VII chords) darken a major song for a moment —
the bittersweet sound of Illenium and of film. A melody that touches ♭6 over iv and falls to 5 is crying.

## 3. The motif and its development

A **motif** is the smallest unit the ear recognises: a rhythm cell (most of its identity) and a shape. Five shapes
cover most motifs: **rise** (3-4-5-8), **fall**, **arch**, **turn** (around a note), **leap and fill** (a leap, then
steps back). Keep a motif to 3–7 notes, within a 6th.

| technique | what | why it works |
|---|---|---|
| repetition | the same | recognition — a hook IS repetition |
| **sequence** | the motif moved by a step or a third | development the ear can follow; rising = building, falling = settling |
| **tonal sequence** | the sequence bent so its strong notes fit the new chord | a copy pasted over a new chord clashes; composers bend |
| variation | the end changed (a step up, a different last note) | news without losing the thread |
| fragmentation | only its first half, repeated | intensity, momentum before a cadence |
| inversion | every interval turned upside down | an answer that is plainly related (call and response) |
| augmentation | every length doubled | the same idea remembered, slower — breakdowns, endings |
| diminution | every length halved | urgency |
| transposition | the whole phrase in another key | a new chapter (the last-drop lift) |

Balance: roughly **two thirds motif-derived, one third contrast or cadence**. All repetition is boring; no
repetition is aimless.

## 4. Phrase forms (8 bars)

| form | plan | for |
|---|---|---|
| **period** | antecedent (4 bars, ends on a half cadence — a question) + consequent (starts the same, ends home — the answer) | verses, choruses, themes: the most "complete" sounding |
| **sentence** | basic idea (1 bar), the idea moved (1 bar), fragments (1 bar), cadence (1 bar) — twice | drive and momentum |
| **AABA** | a line, again, a contrast, the line home | songs, hooks that need a turn |
| **hook loop** | a 2-bar hook, four times; the last time lifted or changed | EDM drops — repetition is the point |
| **call and response** | the motif, its mirror; again a step higher | trap, dialogue, two instruments |
| **climbing sequence** | the motif a step higher each bar, the climax, the fall | builds, climaxes |

## 5. Contour

- **One climax**, the highest note, **once**, about **70 %** through the phrase (the "golden" region, 60–80 %). A
  climax repeated is no longer one. Reach it by a rise over two or three bars.
- **Gap fill**: a leap of a 4th or more is followed by a step back the other way. Two big leaps the same way only when
  outlining a chord (an arpeggio).
- **Steps ≈ 60 %** (50–75 %): mostly steps is singable; leaps give feeling.
- **Range**: about an octave for a hook (9–16 semitones); a verse lower and narrower; a final chorus wider.
- **No tritone leaps**, no leaps over a 10th in a sung line.
- **Variety**: 7 or more different pitches in 8 bars; no note four times in a row unless it is the rhythm hook.
- **Arch** is the default shape of a phrase; **descending** endings settle; **rising** endings ask.

## 6. Rhythm

- The rhythm of the motif is the hook. Keep **2–4 different bar rhythms** in 8 bars: one is monotonous, eight is
  chaos.
- **Syncopation**: a note starting off the beat and lasting over the next beat. Anticipations (the downbeat note a 16th
  or an 8th early), the **3-3-2** (tresillo) grouping, off-beat starts. EDM, trap and pop want some (20–50 % of
  notes); ballads and film little (0–30 %).
- **Rests**: start after the downbeat now and then ("breath"); leave space for the answer.
- **Density** by tempo: half-time drops at 140–150 bpm carry sixteenths; ballads at 80 bpm carry quarters and eighths.
- **The phrase ends long**: the last note held 2 beats or more.

## 7. Cadences

| cadence | melody | harmony | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| full (authentic) | 2 → 1, 7 → 1, 3-2-1 | V → I | an answer, the end |
| half | ends on 2, 5 or 7 | → V | a question |
| plagal | 6 → 5 or 4 → 3 | IV → I (iv → I: bittersweet "amen") | soft, a hymn's close |
| deceptive | 7 → 1 expected, harmony goes elsewhere | V → vi or ♭VI | surprise, "not yet", loss |
| open | a chord tone of the last chord | a loop's last chord | it continues — EDM hooks |

Over a progression that ends on V (vi–IV–I–V), end on V's 3rd or 5th (7 or 2), not the tonic.

## 8. Genre notes

- **Future bass (Illenium, San Holo)**: wide major-key progressions with borrowed iv / ♭VI; melodies with dotted and
  tresillo rhythms, appoggiaturas, add9 colour on downbeats, a late climax, pitch-bent ("wonky") chords; the lead is
  often a vocal chop or a guitar.
- **Progressive house**: IV–I–V–vi or vi–IV–I–V; long sung notes, the climax in the second half, a lift in the last drop.
- **NCS / future house**: a hook of one rhythm every bar, repeated, bright, chord tones, octave jumps.
- **Melodic trap (Trap Nation)**: minor, i–VI–III–VII; sparse calls with rests, off-beat starts, answers falling.
- **Pop ballad**: periods, steps, appoggiaturas, the chorus higher than the verse.
- **Cinematic**: long notes, suspensions, borrowed chords, augmentation, a slow climb.
- **Kawaii**: sixteenth plucks through chord tones, maj7 / add9, high register.
- **Lo-fi**: 7th chords, the melody on 9ths and 7ths, lazy off-beats, space.

## 9. The rubric — each part, and why

`scripts/melody.py score` turns these into numbers so candidates can be ranked (graded preferences, not pass/fail —
a pass/fail rubric once scored every candidate 1.000, a monotonous line among them):

| part | target | why |
|---|---|---|
| chord_fit | strong notes ≈ 100 % chord tones (appoggiaturas count half) | §1 |
| unresolved | no strong non-chord tone without a step resolution | §1 |
| steps | ≈ 62 % | §5 |
| repeats | few (a hook may have some) | §5 variety |
| leaps | recovered, none over a 10th, no tritone | §5 |
| range | ≈ 12 semitones | §5 |
| variety | ≈ 9 pitches | §5 |
| colour | ≈ 80 % of downbeats on a 3rd/5th/7th/9th, not the root | §1 |
| climax | one, at ≈ 72 % | §5 |
| arc | the climax's bars ≈ 5 semitones over the opening's | §5 |
| tendency | the leading tone resolves | §2 |
| cadence | the last note ≈ 2.5 beats | §6, §7 |
| rhythm | ≈ 3 bar rhythms | §6 |
| syncopation | the style's range | §6 |
| motion | direction changes ≈ 45 % of the time | a line that only climbs or only zigzags is dull |

The user's taste (`data/weights.json`) is added on top: liked styles, forms, rhythms and motif shapes get a bonus,
and metrics inside the range of their liked melodies get one too.

## 10. Worked example — Porch Light's theme (demo/storytrack)

E♭ major, eight bars over E♭ – B♭/D – Cm7 – A♭maj7 – A♭m6 – E♭/G – C♭maj7 – B♭sus4→B♭:

| bar | notes | device |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | G A♭ B♭ E♭ (dotted) | the motif: a rise, 3-4-5-8 |
| 2 | D C B♭ | its answer, down by step |
| 3 | E♭ F G C | a sequence of the motif's first steps, turned down at its end (up would pass the climax) |
| 4 | B♭ C E♭ | an appoggiatura: B♭ on the downbeat over A♭maj7, up a step to C |
| 5 | C♭ B♭ A♭ | the sigh: ♭6 → 5 over the borrowed iv |
| 6 | G A♭ B♭ G | the motif returns and reaches up a sixth |
| 7 | B♭ G♭ E♭ | the climax, once, over the borrowed ♭VI |
| 8 | F E♭ D | a 4–3 suspension into V; it ends on the leading tone — a question |

Later it is augmented (the photograph), inverted (the daughter's answer) and transposed a major third up with its
borrowed chords and its sigh gone (acceptance). Theory serving a story.

# What professionals say about future bass sound — and what this skill took from it

The user asked to "learn from professional from Trap Nation first" (2026-10-22). **Trap Nation is a YouTube channel**
— a curator, not a producer. Its future bass is the music it uploads: Illenium, Said The Sky, Dabin, San Holo, Crystal
Skies, MitiS, Nurko, Slushii, Flume's school. Searches found **no production material from Trap Nation itself** and no
technical interview breaking down an Illenium or Said The Sky session. What follows is the craft as professional
producers and their teachers write it down; where a number is ours (tuned by ear or measured), the recipe says so.

## 1. Sources and what each taught

**Native Instruments — "How to make future bass" (Tim Cant)** (https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-make-future-bass/)
- tempo 130–160 bpm (their project 150);
- 7th, 9th, 11th and 13th chords, the root muted in the chord synth;
- the chord synth's VOLUME stepped at 1/16 (step 1 up, step 2 down): the future bass tremolo;
- an eighth-note bassline on the roots;
- half-time drums: closed hats on eighths, three-note 32nd rolls at the end of the second bar;
- the lead layered from five patches — a plucked, acoustic-style lead, one an octave down for sustain, a mid-range
  body, a saw lead with vibrato.

→ 150 bpm half time; the shaper at 1/16 on the supersaw; the main synth as a pluck plus a saw lead with vibrato;
32nd rolls.

**EDMProd — "How to make future bass like Flume"** (https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-future-bass-like-flume/)
- 146 bpm (145–160 typical);
- the kick on the 1s, the snare on the 3s (half time);
- two kick layers, phase-aligned;
- a snare and a clap layered, the snare a semitone up, one reverb for both;
- hats high-passed;
- percussion: off-beat hats, rides, tom fills; a background crash; every second hat 3 dB down;
- bass: a saw and a sine an octave down, low-passed, mono, AM, distortion; a pluck layer high-passed at 200 Hz; an
  octave layer;
- the bass group side-chained with ratio ∞ and a 10 ms release (a ghost trigger);
- the build in two halves (melodic, then hyped), impacts and sweeps in the second half, a snare pitching up an octave;
- "end with a silence before the drop, filled only by a few drum hits and a massive clap".

→ the snare and clap a semitone up with a shared room; the tom fills, the ride and the crash; the mid bass
high-passed (160 Hz); the short duck release; the build's two halves, its octave-climbing snare roll and the silence
with one clap.

**Loopmasters — "How to make a future bass drop"**
(https://www.loopmasters.com/articles/4372-How-To-Make-A-Future-Bass-Drop-A-Future-Bass-Production-Tutorial)
- the supersaw is two 16-voice detuned saw oscillators an octave apart, with a quick pitch envelope UP at the note's
  start;
- a sine bassline on the lowest notes, with a second bass layer chorused and high-passed so it does not crowd the
  sub;
- the synths and the bass ducked by the kick AND the snare;
- fills at the end of every other bar, never over the snare;
- before the drop the drums cut, a white-noise riser, an impact on the drop.

→ the supersaw's two stacks an octave apart and its pitch rise into each stab (the bend lane); the snare's duck on
the chords; the tom fills after the snare; the drums' cut, the riser and the impact.

**Production Music Live — "Future bass drop, start to finish"**
(https://www.productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/futurebass_drop_starttofinish)
- the bass keeps the main synth's root notes: a sine, a square an octave down, a saw, low-pass 4-pole, tube
  distortion;
- the main synth side-chained to the kick (ratio ~6), the same on the bass;
- gaps in the chords after the half-bar so the snare hits.

→ the mid bass's tube drive; the bass on the chord roots.

**Feelyoursound — "Future bass tutorial"** (https://feelyoursound.com/articles/future-bass-tutorial/)
- major 7 and minor 7 chords everywhere;
- the bass plays long notes while the chord instrument plays short rhythmic ones;
- "wobbly" synths, the filter opened and closed quickly.

**Unison — "How to make future bass"** (https://unison.audio/how-to-make-future-bass/)
- a supersaw heavily detuned, a square under the saws, an LFO on the filter;
- a pitch modulation on note attacks for a vocal-like quality;
- sub and mid bass layered, both side-chained;
- closed-hat rolls with open hats.

**Reese bass** — Attack Magazine (https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/fred-again-inspired-reese-basslines-with-twin-3/),
Native Instruments (https://blog.native-instruments.com/reese-bass/), Soundbridge
(https://www.soundbridge.io/design-a-reese-bass-from-scratch), dnbforum (https://dnbforum.com/threads/how-is-the-reese-effect-done.185969/):
- two detuned saws, filtered: the slight detune makes them cancel and reinforce in turn (the "phasing" movement);
- about ±25 cents is a common start, ±7 cents is subtler;
- a sine sub an octave under for weight;
- mono and legato, with glide.

→ two saws ±12 cents (the unison pair), a sine an octave down, legato with a 70 ms glide, and a slow phaser for more
movement.

**FM bell** — Splice, "How to create a silver bell sound using FM synthesis" (https://splice.com/blog/?p=7958):
- a sine carrier detuned slightly;
- a modulator at a NON-integer ratio: the inharmonic, metallic partials of a bell;
- a quick attack and a long ring-out.

→ the FM synth, algorithm 5: op2 at ratio 3.5 into op1, and an octave pair (op4 into op3, op3 7 cents sharp) for
shimmer; the clang fading in 650 ms, the tone in 2.6 s. "Airy" is ours: a 600 Hz high-pass, a dotted-8th echo, a
4.5 s room, and the register (D6–D7).

**Build-ups** — Soundbridge, "Make massive layered build-ups" (https://soundbridge.io/make-massive-layered-build-ups),
MusicRadar, "Synth sweep FX" (https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/synth-sweep-fx), Computer Music:
- the snare roll from sixteenths with its velocity ramping to maximum;
- white noise through a low-pass whose cutoff opens, its level and reverb rising;
- a pitch riser of 12–24 semitones over the build;
- the layers combined, not used separately.

→ the snare roll (quarters → 32nds, velocity 70 → 120), the noise riser (300 Hz → 12 kHz, −40 → −8 dB), the pitch
riser (+24 semitones over four bars).

**Drums** — MusicRadar, "How to program the perfect drum roll" (https://musicradar.com/how-to/how-to-program-the-perfect-drum-roll-part-2):
- rolls are not only for the snare (hats too);
- vary the velocity between hits.

## 2. What is OURS (not from a source)

- **The kick tuned to the key's fifth** (A1, 55 Hz, for D major), from the DSP library's measured kick law (48·2^(tune/12) Hz).
- **The bassline IS the kick's rhythm.** The user asked for "kick and bassline sync": every kick strikes the bass.
- **The kick patterns.** Bar A strikes 1, the and of 2, and the a of 3 (right after the snare: the bounce). Bar B
  strikes 1, the a of 1, the and of 3, and the and of 4 (pushing into the next bar).
- **The duck's release.** 70 ms on the bass and 120 ms on the chords, from a measurement: Solaris's default half-beat
  release held the sub down ~600 ms behind the kick's tail.
- **The supersaw voicing.** The melody is the top note of every stab, with the chord's three nearest tones under it.
- **The space in each element's rack**, so a solo is honest.
- **The levels.** Every element is measured against the order in SKILL.md §2.6.
